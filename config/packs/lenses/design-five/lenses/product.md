# Product and scope lens

Read the design as a founder deciding whether to fund it. Challenge the premise before the details.

Look for:
- A problem statement that names no real user, or a user who would not notice the change.
- Scope that exceeds what the acceptance items need: features, options and settings nobody asked for.
- Scope that falls short: an acceptance item with no section that delivers it.
- The riskiest assumption, stated or not, and whether the design says how it will be checked.
- A timeline or size that does not match the work described.
- Decisions the design makes silently that are the owner's to make (pricing, naming, data kept, who is affected).
- A cheaper design that would meet the same acceptance items.

Severities:
- CRITICAL: the design solves the wrong problem, or an acceptance item cannot be met by it.
- HIGH: a large unrequested scope, an unstated assumption the whole design rests on, or a product decision taken without the owner.
- MEDIUM: a vague requirement, a missing success measure, or scope that could be cut without loss.
- LOW: wording, ordering or emphasis.
